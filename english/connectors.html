Ultimate English Connectors & Conjunctions Guide

The 3-Tier Classification Rule (The Core Architecture)
  • Conjunctions: Glue two clauses together inside one sentence → + Subject + Verb
  • Prepositions: Link an idea to an object inside one sentence → + Noun / -ing
  • Conjunctive Adverbs: Cannot join two clauses with just a comma. Must finish the previous clause with a period (.) or semicolon (;), followed by Word, + Subject + Verb.

1. Contrast & Concession

Category Word / Phrase Structure Example
Conjunction Although / Even though + Subject + Verb (Fact) "Although it was raining, we went outside."
Conjunction Even if + Subject + Verb (Hypothesis) "We will go outside even if it rains."
Preposition Despite / In spite of + Noun / -ing "Despite the heavy rain, we went outside."
Conjunctive Adverb Nevertheless / Nonetheless / However . Word, + Subject + Verb "It rained heavily. Nevertheless, we went outside."
* Note: Never write despite of (use either despite or in spite of).

2. Cause, Reason & Consequence

Category Word / Phrase Structure Example
Conjunction Because / Since / As + Subject + Verb "We stayed home because it rained."
Preposition Because of / Due to / Owing to + Noun / -ing "We stayed home because of the rain."
Conjunctive Adverb Therefore / Thus / Consequently . Word, + Subject + Verb "It rained heavily. Therefore, we stayed home."

3. Time, Duration & Parallelism

Category Word / Phrase Structure Example
Conjunction While / As + Subject + Verb "I fell asleep while I was watching the movie."
Preposition During + Specific Period / Event "I fell asleep during the movie."
Preposition For + Numeric Duration "I slept for three hours."

4. Condition & Contingency

Function Conjunction (+ Subject + Verb) Preposition (+ Noun / -ing)
Negative Condition ("If not") Unless you sign, it is void. Without your signature, it is void.
Exclusion ("Except") Great, except that it rained. Great, except for the rain.
Precautionary Action Call 119 in case there is fire. Call 119 in case of fire.
Conditional Alternative Provided that / As long as you pay... —

5. Purpose & Replacement

Category Word / Phrase Structure Example
Conjunction So that / In order that + S + can/could + V "I hurried so that I could catch the bus."
Infinitive In order to / So as to + Base Verb "I hurried in order to catch the bus."
Preposition Instead of + Noun / -ing "He rested instead of working."
Conjunctive Adverb Instead . Word, + S + V "He did not work. Instead, he rested."

6. Comparison & Context

Function Conjunction (+ Clause) Preposition (+ Noun)
Side-by-side Contrast Seoul is fast, whereas Jeju is calm. Unlike Seoul, Jeju is calm.
Context / Premise Given that he was new, he did well. Given his background, he did well.
Manner / Likeness Do it as I instructed. He moves like a dancer.

7. Correlative Conjunctions (Paired Forms)

Pair Pattern Meaning Subject-Verb Agreement Rule Example
Both A and B Both items included Always takes a plural verb "Both the manager and the staff were friendly."
Either A or B One of the two Verb agrees with element B "Either you or he is responsible."
Neither A nor B Neither of the two Verb agrees with element B "Neither the plan nor the budget was approved."
Not only A but also B Both (with emphasis on B) Verb agrees with element B "Not only he but also his friends were late."
Not A but B Only B, excluding A Verb agrees with element B "It is not a bug, but a feature."

8. Noun Clause Markers

Word Clause Requirement Core Meaning Example
That + Complete Clause (S + V + O) Statement of an established fact "I know that she told the truth."
Whether / If + Complete Clause (S + V + O) Expression of doubt or alternatives "I doubt whether he will show up."
What (Relative Pronoun) + Incomplete Clause (Missing S or O) "The thing(s) that" (internal antecedent) "I heard what she said."
* Quick Check: If the following clause is grammatically complete, use that. If it lacks a subject or an object, use what.
